- Shorter windows reduce structural-break inflation (break passes through faster) BUT increase sampling-noise variance
- Longer windows reduce sampling-noise variance BUT extend duration of structural-break contamination
- Trade-off is bias-variance classical, with 40 periods near the sweet spot
- 20-period window: estimator drops below 0.60 in 4 reviews on phi=0.85 stationary data due to sample noise
- 40-period window: structural break contaminates exactly 2 reviews (40 periods / 20 review interval = 2)
- 100-period window: structural break contaminates 5 reviews (100 / 20 = 5)

CONCLUSION: OLS structural-break inflation is a FUNDAMENTAL LIMITATION not fixable by window-size tuning. Better fixes would require:
  - Explicit break detection (CUSUM, change-point detection)
  - Break-robust estimators (Yule-Walker, recursive OLS with break re-initialization)
  - Median-based robust regression

These are deferred to future work. For Paper 9, document the limitation honestly with this diagnostic as supporting evidence.

PUBLICATION FRAMING: "Phi-gating discriminates persistent from transient demand shifts on stationary or near-stationary demand series. The standard OLS estimator's known sensitivity to structural breaks inflates the persistence estimate during pricing reviews where the estimation window straddles a regime change, partially defeating the discrimination capability for the few reviews immediately following such a break. Empirical testing of window-size variants confirms the 40-period window strikes the best balance between break-inflation duration and sampling-noise variance."

RECIPE-LEVEL NON-STATIONARITY FINDING - DISCOVERED VIA THREE-VARIANT DIAGNOSTIC IN CHAIN-LENGTH SWEEP (May 1, 2026)

This is a NEW finding distinct from the OLS structural-break limitation already documented in the Phase 2.7 supplement.

THE FINDING:
In drift_canonical environment (where true phi changes through time: 0.3 -> 0.95 -> 0.4), the three estimator variants behave dramatically differently at L=8 cap=2.4x:

   sr_paper9_ols (real estimator):   -0.116% (mild benefit)
   sr_oracle_local (perfect phi):    +0.199% (HARMS)
   sr_naive_damp (fixed alpha):      -1.313% (large benefit)

The Oracle has zero estimation error - she is handed the true phi value at every moment. Yet she harms in drift_canonical at every cell tested (range +0.20% to +0.41% across all 9 cells). Meanwhile naive_damp, which uses no estimator and no formula at all (just a fixed alpha), produces large benefits in the longer chains.

DISCRIMINATING LOGIC:
If the problem were OLS estimator slowness, the Oracle would fix it (she has no estimator). She doesn't fix it - she makes it worse. Therefore the problem is not in the estimator. The problem is in the recipe itself - the alpha-from-phi mapping derived from spectral-radius stability in Paper 7.

MECHANISM HYPOTHESIS:
The recipe was derived for stationary phi. It assumes the chain will face today's phi value indefinitely. In drift_canonical phi is mid-trajectory at every moment - climbing from 0.3 toward 0.95, then descending toward 0.4. The recipe takes today's phi and produces the alpha that would be optimal IF phi were going to stay at this value forever. That alpha is wrong for a world where phi is in flight. The Oracle exhibits this perfectly: she reads the trajectory accurately but her recipe can't use trajectory information, only point information. Naive_damp sidesteps the issue by never deriving anything from phi. Paper 9 OLS partially compensates because OLS lag accidentally gives it a smoothed phi reading that's closer to a fixed alpha than the Oracle's crisp reading - a defect of the estimator partially offsetting a defect of the recipe.

DISTINCTION FROM PHASE 2.7 OLS STRUCTURAL-BREAK FINDING:
Phase 2.7 found that OLS inflates phi above 0.75 during structural breaks, defeating the symmetric phi-gating threshold. That is an estimator problem - Yule-Walker or change-point detection could fix it. The new finding is a recipe problem - even Yule-Walker would not fix it because the Oracle (which has no estimator) also harms.

SUPPLEMENT IMPACT:
Phase 2.7 framing was: 'phi-gating discriminates persistent from transient shifts WHEN demand series has no structural break in estimation window'. This framing assumed the recipe was fine and the estimator was the bottleneck. The new finding requires a refinement: the formula has TWO non-stationarity weaknesses - estimator-level (already documented) and recipe-level (new). The recipe-level weakness CANNOT be fixed by improving the estimator. It requires deriving a new alpha-from-phi recipe that takes both the level of phi and the rate of change of phi as inputs.

GENERALITY UNVERIFIED:

SR_NUMERICAL VARIANT INVESTIGATION - RESOLVED (May 1, 2026)

The sr_numerical variant in the chain-length sweep returned exactly zero in all nine cells, four environments, fifty seeds. WHAT SR_NUMERICAL ACTUALLY IS:
sr_numerical is NOT "an alternative numerical estimator" as the original test plan and supplement draft described it. It uses the same OLS estimator as sr_paper9_ols. The difference between sr_numerical and sr_paper9_ols is in the DAMPING_MODE, not the estimator. Specifically:
- sr_paper9_ols uses DAMPING_PAPER9 mode = compute_alpha_pi_squared_over_two with k_star=0.90 default
- sr_numerical uses DAMPING_NUMERICAL mode = compute_alpha_numerical with k_star=1.00 default
- The numerical rule uses pi^2/S (without the /2 factor) and no safety margin
- The numerical rule produces alpha values roughly 2x larger than Paper 9's, which means they almost always exceed 1.0 and get clipped at 1.0 (no damping engagement)

WHY THIS IS DOCUMENTATION-WORKING-AS-DESIGNED:
The code documentation in phase2_6_spectral_radius.py compute_alpha_numerical docstring states explicitly: "The SR-numerical variant exists specifically to document that the threshold change matters. We expect this variant to rarely engage damping at typical persistence levels."

This variant was preserved in the codebase after the April 22 correction specifically to document the pre-correction behavior. Before April 22, the simulator used the numerical stability threshold rho<=1.0 instead of the closed-form Paper 7 pi^2/2 bound. The numerical threshold produces alpha values that exceed 1.0 across nearly the entire persistence range, which means the gadget effectively did nothing in most scenarios.

The variant's zero results are themselves the demonstration of why the April 22 correction was necessary.

YULE-WALKER ESTIMATOR COMPARISON - INVESTIGATED AND CLOSED (May 1, 2026)

The Yule-Walker estimator was investigated as a candidate alternative to the OLS estimator used throughout Paper 9. The reviewer concern this addresses is the well-known Hurwicz bias of OLS on finite-sample AR(1) processes. JAE directed that this be a focused investigation rather than a deep rabbit hole, because the Oracle variant already shows the formula's bottleneck is at the recipe level, not the estimator level.

EMPIRICAL FINDING:
A controlled small-sample bias comparison was run with 200 synthetic AR(1) histories at true phi=0.95 and W=40 (matching the chain-length sweep configuration). Results:
- OLS mean estimate: 0.835 (downward biased by 0.115 from true 0.95)
- Yule-Walker mean estimate: 0.803 (downward biased by 0.147 from true 0.95)
- Yule-Walker is MORE downward-biased than OLS at our conditions, by ~0.032

MECHANICAL REASON:
Both estimators have the same numerator (sum of cross-products of consecutive centered observations). Yule-Walker has a larger denominator that includes the squared deviation of the final observation, while OLS excludes it. Larger denominator means smaller fraction. Since OLS is already downward biased, Yule-Walker being mechanically smaller pushes it further downward, not upward. The textbook claim that Yule-Walker has "improved small-sample properties" applies to specific bias-correction conventions that do not match the unmodified estimator we tested.

WHY WE'RE NOT GOING DEEPER:
The Oracle variant (which uses no estimator at all and gets true phi handed to it directly) ALREADY harms in drift_canonical at every cell tested in the chain-length sweep. The Oracle is the upper bound on what any estimator could achieve. No estimator improvement can exceed the Oracle's performance, and the Oracle's performance is already documented to be insufficient in non-stationary regimes. The formula's improvement room is at the recipe level, not the estimator level. Spending time exploring bias-corrected variants (Marriott-Pope, Andrews, unbiased Yule-Walker, etc.) would be testing different paint when the wall has structural damage. Recipe-level fix is reserved for the synthesis paper that combines Papers 2, 5, 7, and 9.

WHAT GOT WRITTEN UP:
Finding twenty-three has been added to the supplement at /mnt/user-data/outputs/Paper9_Supplement_New_Sections_Findings_20_21.md.
