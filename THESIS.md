## The claim

Control theory answers whether a feedback system is stable at its current parameters. This paper asks what happens, and what it costs, DURING the transition into instability - while the persistence of a measured variable is drifting upward, the closed-loop spectral radius is approaching and crossing the stability boundary, and a backward-looking estimator has not yet detected the change.

The setup: a managed variable y (for example a firm's inventory-to-sales ratio) has autoregressive persistence phi; a trailing average of window W estimates it; a feedback policy of strength beta*gamma adjusts the system off that estimate; the closed loop is a W x W companion matrix whose spectral radius rho determines stability (rho < 1, perturbations decay; rho > 1, they compound). At a regime change the true persistence steps up from phi_1 to phi_2, moving the system from stable (rho_1 < 1) to unstable (rho_2 > 1). The estimator does not detect the change immediately; it converges to the new persistence over an adaptation time tau that is a structural function of W (longer windows give more accurate steady-state estimates but longer blind periods).

The central claim - the **Measurement Damage Theorem**: **the damage accumulated during the blind period is bounded by D = (rho_2/rho_1)^tau** - an INTENSITY factor (the spectral-radius ratio, how fast errors amplify per cycle) raised to a DURATION (the adaptation time, how many cycles the system operates blind), both functions of the same design parameter W. Two consequences follow. First, a unique optimal measurement window W* minimizes expected total cost by balancing estimation accuracy (favoring long W) against adaptation speed (favoring short W), computable in closed form via the Lambert W function. Second, operating at the pi^2/2 speed limit is suboptimal under regime-change risk because the limit is calibrated to the estimated (lagging) persistence, not the true persistence; the optimal operating point is a fraction k* < 1 of the limit.

The claim has an operational, testable second half: **the damage metric D, computed from data a firm already holds, has out-of-sample predictive content for the realized excess deviation a system suffers when its regime shifts** - and, at the firm level, the sign of (rho - 1) predicts whether the next shock produces amplifying or decaying oscillations. This is the falsifiable core (see The falsifier) and the seed of the forward prediction.

Source-manuscript values to be re-earned (source material, not inherited): rolling out-of-sample significance in 11 of 17 sectors (Spearman +0.16 to +0.36; mean +0.157); GFC episode Spearman +0.456 (p ~ 0.066, n = 17), with the combined D metric ranking at least comparably to the component predictors (rho alone +0.31; |Delta phi| and tau each +0.39); a non-significant COVID episode (rho ~ -0.13) interpreted as a boundary condition (persistence fell rather than rose); Beer Game Monte Carlo mean costs (naive ~ $60.9M; ERP ~ $7.5M; spectral-radius tool ~ $5.2M, ~30% below ERP; full theorem ~ $5.1M, a further ~1.8%, 98.4% pairwise win); the 17-sector instability ranking with computers/electronics manufacturing and wholesale machinery each rho > 1 in 42.9% of months; the capacity-utilization stability threshold (mean rho crossing 1.0 near 85-90% utilization); k* ~ 0.85-0.95 for manufacturing parameters; and the additional-simulation findings (chain-length harm-to-benefit crossover under all-tier deployment; a pricing asymmetry in which cutting prices in response to demand decreases is uniformly negative across tested environments; hysteresis robustness that splits by capacity/persistence regime). The recipe-level non-stationarity limitation (the alpha-from-phi damping recipe loses value under non-stationary persistence even with perfect knowledge) is carried as a documented limitation, not a headline result.

## Why it matters

The steady-state stability of measurement-driven feedback systems is well studied; the transient cost of measurement lag during a regime change is not treated in the form developed here. Any institution that drives a feedback response off a smoothed, backward-looking measurement of a persistent variable is exposed to this cost, and the theorem makes it computable from quantities such institutions already estimate: the persistence of the managed variable, the effective measurement window, and the feedback aggressiveness. The result is a reusable diagnostic - a speedometer (rho), a speed limit (pi^2/2), a reaction time (tau), and a bound on the crash damage (D) - rather than a single-domain finding.

The stakes are concrete. In supply chains the diagnostic identifies which sectors sit chronically at or above the instability boundary; in this dataset the two most structurally unstable sectors are the two the roughly $500 billion CHIPS Act depends on, and the capacity-utilization threshold offers a monitoring benchmark for the build-out. The same measurement-feedback structure appears, more tentatively, in sovereign credit ratings (a downgrade raises borrowing costs, which worsens fundamentals, which can trigger further downgrades) and in U.S. state unemployment-insurance tax formulas (experience rating that steepens procyclically, raising the marginal cost of a layoff during recessions). The paper presents the supply-chain results as validated and the sovereign/UI extensions as suggestive, inviting further investigation.

## The gap

Four literatures bear on this question; the gap sits precisely between them.

1. **Control-theoretic supply-chain stability (the Cardiff school).** Disney and Towill (2002, 2003), Dejonckheere et al. (2003, 2004), Disney (2008), Ouyang and Daganzo (2006), Boute et al. (2006), Gaalman-Disney-Wang (2022), Li-Gaalman-Disney (2023) established z-transform / pole-location / H-infinity stability analysis of ordering policies - mathematically rho(A) < 1. Several foundational closed-form results (including the Disney-Towill-Van de Velde (2004) golden-ratio gain, and Warburton-Disney (2007) using the Lambert W for pure time delays) were derived under i.i.d. demand, where the instability mechanism this paper studies does not arise. This tradition answers "is the system stable at its current parameters?" - not "how much damage accumulates during the transition?" The paper builds on this foundation and makes no priority claim over rho(A) < 1.

2. **Transient and nonlinear supply-chain dynamics.** Spiegler et al. (2016) and Lin et al. (2020) analyze step-response, drift, and nonlinear transients directly - establishing that transient behavior is a real object of study - but do not supply a closed-form bound tying a backward-looking estimator's adaptation time to a regime transition.

3. **Recent neighboring closed-form transient work.** Li and Dorfler (2024) define a transient bullwhip measure with explicit forecast-error dependence via robust control (a peak-gain / ell-infinity optimization solved with bilinear matrix inequalities, for a single vendor restabilizing around a steady state; forecast error modeled as a bounded disturbance). Leng, Liu, Ren, and Tsyvinski (2025, NBER WP 33638) derive a closed-form general-equilibrium production-network model in which hump-shaped (persistent) demand shocks amplify upstream while monotone-decay shocks do not. Both are genuinely close and are positioned explicitly; both differ from this paper's object (a structural trailing-average adaptation time and the damage its lag produces, framed as a transition between two persistence regimes rather than disturbance rejection or fluctuation around a stable mean).

4. **Adaptive-control transient bounds.** Krstic-Kokotovic (1993), Datta-Ioannou (1994), Zang-Bitmead (1994), Gibson-Annaswamy-Lavretsky (2013) bound transient performance as a function of adaptation gain. The adaptation-speed / transient-quality tradeoff is established prior art and is cited as such. The distinction is one of object: adaptive control studies an actively designed controller whose adaptation speed is a TUNABLE design gain; the adaptation time tau here is a STRUCTURAL, non-tunable property of a backward-looking trailing average, dictated by the same window length that also sets its estimation accuracy - which is the tension the theorem is built to resolve.

**Why the gap survives.** To our knowledge no prior work combines a backward-looking estimator's structural adaptation time with a regime-transition spectral-radius ratio into the closed-form damage bound D = (rho_2/rho_1)^tau, validates the resulting metric out-of-sample across a multi-sector panel, and extends the same construction across supply chains, the CHIPS-Act sectors, sovereign credit ratings, and unemployment insurance. The switched-systems joint-spectral-radius transient bounds of Plischke-Wirth (2008) and Jungers (2009) are superficially similar but apply to arbitrary switching between abstract matrices, whereas our transition follows the estimator's convergence with tau a function of W. A fresh 2026 web pass (recorded below) found nothing that closes the gap.

## The falsifier (single, concrete, observable)

**Primary falsifier (runnable by anyone from public U.S. Census I/S data plus the committed code).** Run the rolling out-of-sample test across the 17-sector panel: at each sector-month, estimate persistence from the trailing 60 months and regime change from the trailing 12, compute the predicted damage metric D using only backward-looking data, and correlate it with the SUBSEQUENT 12-month inventory-to-sales deviation. The thesis is FALSIFIED if the predicted damage carries no out-of-sample predictive content - specifically, if a MAJORITY of the sectors that oscillate between stability regimes fail to show a statistically significant positive Spearman correlation (p < 0.01) between predicted D and realized I/S deviation, or if the sign of the association across the panel is predominantly negative. Either outcome would show the damage bound has no empirical predictive power, contradicting the operational half of the central claim. (The source manuscript's result - 11 of 17 sectors significant, with one chronically-unstable sector showing the theorem's own boundary condition - is source material, to be re-earned; it is NOT assumed here.)

The theoretical half is falsified independently at Phase 3: the Measurement Damage Theorem D = (rho_2/rho_1)^tau and its optimal-window and safety-factor results must pass a committed symbolic step-check and a numeric stress test; a written proof that does not hold, or a check that does not resolve true, reopens the build and is itself a falsification of the theorem as stated.

**Post-publication falsifier (dated forward prediction, finalized in Phase 4 and registered publicly at Phase 5c).** The firm-level self-service diagnostic yields a standing, refutable prediction that routes around both author and reviewer: a firm (or sector) computes its own spectral radius rho from its inventory-to-sales history; the prediction is that a system with rho > 1 will respond to its next demand shock with amplifying (growing) I/S oscillations, and a system with rho < 1 with decaying oscillations. Falsified for a case if a system with rho > 1 systematically exhibits decaying oscillations after a genuine shock (or vice versa). Exact thresholds, the observation window, and the reporting protocol are fixed when the paper is written; an interactive calculator accompanies the paper so a reader can run the test.

- **Transient / nonlinear supply-chain dynamics stream:** Spiegler-Potter-Naim-Towill 2016; Lin-Naim-Spiegler 2020; Udenio-Fransoo-Peels 2015 (GFC destocking); Boute-Disney-Gijsbrechts-Van Mieghem 2022 (non-stationary/IMA demand). - **Recent closest-cousin closed-form transient stream:** Li-Dorfler 2024 (transient bullwhip via robust control / peak gain, single vendor, bounded forecast error); Leng-Liu-Ren-Tsyvinski 2025 (NBER WP 33638, closed-form production-network amplification by shock persistence). Verdict: closest work; different object and framing; positioned explicitly.
- **Adaptive-control transient-bound stream:** Krstic-Kokotovic 1993; Datta-Ioannou 1994; Zang-Bitmead 1994; Gibson-Annaswamy-Lavretsky 2013; Haykin 1996 (adaptive filtering). Verdict: adaptation-speed/transient-quality tradeoff is acknowledged prior art; tau here is structural (window-set), not a tunable design gain.
- **Switched-systems joint-spectral-radius stream:** Plischke-Wirth 2008; Jungers 2009; Helbing-Lammer-Witt-Brenner 2004 (statistical-physics oscillation). Verdict: transient JSR bounds for arbitrary switching between abstract matrices; our transition follows the estimator's convergence, parameterized by observables.
- **Empirical bullwhip stream:** Cachon-Randall-Schmidt 2007; Bray-Mendelson 2012/2015; Shan et al. 2014; Dooley et al. 2010; Saricioglu et al. 2025; Chen-Drezner-Ryan-Simchi-Levi 2000 (variance amplification; managed-variable conditioning); Hosoda-Disney 2006 (uneven echelon amplification). Verdict: variance-ratio point estimates, not a time-varying rho > 1 stability fraction.
- **Minsky-in-operations stream:** Oliva-Sterman 2001; Repenning-Sterman 2001/2002; Rasmussen 1997; Dekker 2011. Verdict: stability-breeds-instability dynamics; the spectral radius as distance-to-boundary and the damage theorem for crossing are the contribution here.
- **Semiconductor / CHIPS / Factory Physics stream:** Hopp-Spearman 2008 (VUT knee at 85-90%); Monch-Fowler-Dauzere-Peres 2011; Anderson-Fine-Parker 2000; Tziantzioulis/Ning et al. 2023 (Chip Agility Score); Nepal-Murat-Chinnam 2012 (capacity attenuates bullwhip). - **Cross-domain streams:** Ferri-Liu-Stiglitz 1999 (rating procyclicality); Jorda-Schularick-Taylor Macrohistory (sovereign persistence); Anderson-Meyer 1994, Woodbury 2004, Fath-Fuest 2002 (UI experience rating); Werner 1997/2005/2014a/b (directed credit). Verdict: motivate the cross-domain extensions, presented as suggestive.
- **Fresh 2026 web pass (this session, 2026-07-13):** targeted queries on transient-bullwhip measurement-lag regime-transition damage, closed-form damage bounds tying trailing-average adaptation time to persistence regime change, and spectral-radius supply-chain stability returned Li-Dorfler 2024 (already positioned), 2026 simulation/benchmarking toolkits (e.g. open-source multi-echelon bullwhip simulators that note regime switching but supply no closed-form bound), reinforcement-learning / machine-learning inventory and demand-forecasting studies, production-smoothing empirics, and adaptive-control step-size and adjacent-domain (robotics EMA stabilizers, agent-based governance-delay) work. None computes D = (rho_2/rho_1)^tau tying a structural estimator adaptation time to a regime-transition spectral-radius ratio, validates it across a sector panel, or extends the construction cross-domain. Gap confirmed to survive as of 2026-07-13.

**Significance:** the result converts an established steady-state stability tradition into a transient-cost diagnostic that is computable from data institutions already hold, connects previously parallel literatures (bullwhip stability and Factory Physics capacity theory) through an empirical persistence channel, and bears on a live $500 billion industrial-policy build-out. The gap is real and closing it advances the frontier. PROCEED.
